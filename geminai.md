# 30 Things to Avoid in the Web App

## Design & UI Anti-Patterns

1. **Harsh gradients**
   Avoid aggressive gradient backgrounds. Use a restrained navy, white, neutral.

2. **Icons everywhere**
   Use icons only when they improve recognition or navigation. Do not decorate every card with an icon.

3. **Pure white backgrounds everywhere**
   Use subtle neutral surfaces and carefully separated content areas to reduce visual fatigue.

4. **Rainbow risk colours**
   Do not use excessive red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple simultaneously. Risk states should have a consistent semantic system.

5. **Excessive drop shadows**
   Prefer borders, spacing, and subtle elevation over heavy shadows.

6. **Generic three-card feature sections**
   Do not repeatedly use three identical cards for every section. Use layouts that match the actual information being presented.

7. **Emojis as UI elements**
   Do not use emojis for navigation, alerts, analytics, or professional system controls.

8. **Liquid glass / excessive glassmorphism**
   Avoid translucent floating panels that reduce readability and make the system look like a consumer AI app.

9. **Excessive em dashes**
   Keep the interface copy natural, concise, and professional.

10. **Overused fonts such as Inter, Geist, or Space Grotesk**
    Choose a highly readable professional typeface with strong accessibility and institutional character.

11. **Decorative coloured left stripes**
    Do not add coloured stripes to cards merely for visual decoration. Colour should communicate meaningful status or hierarchy.

12. **Fake testimonials**
    Never invent victim, counsellor, official, government, or institutional testimonials.

13. **Overused bento grids**
    Do not force every feature into a collection of floating rectangular cards.

14. **Generic terminal/code windows**
    Avoid fake developer-console visuals. SAHAYA-AI is a public welfare and support platform, not a developer tool.

15. **Generic AI marketing clichés**
    Avoid copy such as “Revolutionising the future with next-generation AI.” Use precise language describing what the system actually does.

16. **Checkmark bullets everywhere**
    Use normal typography, numbered steps, icons, or structured content where appropriate.

17. **Generic pricing sections**
    SAHAYA-AI is not a SaaS subscription product. Do not include pricing tiers.

18. **No real product demonstration**
    The website must demonstrate the actual workflow: victim check-in → AI analysis → distress indicators → explainable alert → human review → intervention → follow-up.

19. **Excessively soft / rounded corners**
    Avoid making every element a large pill or heavily rounded card. Use restrained corner radii.

20. **Purple-and-black “AI aesthetic”**
    Although `#702186` is the primary brand colour, do not turn the entire application into a purple-and-black futuristic AI interface. Use navy, neutral surfaces, white, and purple strategically.

21. **Missing skeleton loaders**
    Use skeleton states when dashboards, case information, charts, conversations, or analysis results are loading.

22. **Decorative radial orbs**
    Avoid glowing background circles and decorative AI lighting effects that have no functional purpose.

23. **Dot-grid backgrounds**
    Do not use generic dot-grid or technical-grid backgrounds simply to make the application appear “AI-powered.”

24. **Random sparkle icons**
    Avoid sparkle/star icons commonly associated with generic AI interfaces unless they communicate a specific function.

25. **Unnecessary animated arrows**
    Use animation only when it explains a workflow, transition, progress state, or system status.

26. **Missing Terms of Service**
    Include appropriate Terms of Service / platform-use information for the prototype.

27. **Missing Privacy Policy**
    Privacy must be a visible first-class part of the application because the platform handles sensitive victim-related information.

28. **Excessive hover animations**
    Keep interactions subtle and purposeful. Avoid cards jumping, floating, rotating, or glowing unnecessarily.

29. **Neon colours without purpose**
    Use strong colours only when they communicate meaningful system states such as alerts or status.

30. **Basic pastel healthcare aesthetic**
    Avoid making the application look like a generic wellness or meditation app. SAHAYA-AI should feel like a serious public-sector technology platform.

# General Rule

Avoid combining multiple common **AI SaaS, healthcare dashboard, or generic chatbot design clichés**.

Every visual and interaction element must have a clear purpose.

The application should feel:

* **Purpose-built**
* **Professional**
* **Victim-centred**
* **Trustworthy**
* **Government-ready**
* **Privacy-conscious**
* **Human-centred**
* **Explainable**
* **Data-driven**
* **Accessible**
* **Calm but not clinical**
* **Secure**
* **Real rather than AI-generated**

# Victim Portal Design Principles

The Victim Portal must feel:

**Safe → Calm → Private → Supportive → Simple**

Do not make the victim feel as though they are being monitored by a surveillance system.

Avoid:

* Large red “HIGH RISK” indicators
* Fear-inducing warnings
* Complex analytics
* Technical AI terminology
* Excessive charts
* Unnecessary case statistics
* Forced camera access

Prioritize:

* Conversational check-ins
* Supportive language
* Clear privacy information
* Easy access to human support
* Simple progress/history
* Optional voice and video interaction
* Clear ability to stop or exit a check-in

---

# Counsellor / Official Portal Design Principles

The Official Portal should feel:

**Analytical → Structured → Action-oriented → Explainable**

Prioritize:

* Case overview
* Well-being trends
* AI-generated indicators
* Explainable contributing signals
* Alert prioritisation
* Human review
* Intervention tracking
* Follow-up scheduling

Do not show unexplained AI scores.

Instead of:

> Risk Score: 82

show:

> **Well-being indicator: 82**
> **Trend: Increasing**
> **Reason for review:** Increased distress-related signals compared with previous interactions.

---

# Administrator Portal Design Principles

The Administrator Portal should feel:

**Institutional → Controlled → Transparent → Data-driven**

Prioritize:

* User management
* Role-based permissions
* System analytics
* Case distribution
* Audit logs
* Access monitoring
* System configuration

Avoid exposing unnecessary victim-level information to administrators unless their role requires it.

---

# AI & Safety Design Principles

SAHAYA-AI must never visually imply that AI can diagnose a victim.

Avoid:

> “Depression detected”

> “Emotion detected with 95% accuracy”

> “Victim is mentally unstable”

> “AI has determined the victim is high risk”

Prefer:

> **Potential distress indicator identified**

> **Well-being trend is increasing**

> **AI-generated signal requires human review**

> **Additional support may be appropriate**

> **Human assessment required**

# Core Product Workflow

The actual product demonstration should be the centre of the application:

```text
Victim Check-in
      ↓
Text / Voice / Optional Video
      ↓
Signal Analysis
      ↓
Historical Comparison
      ↓
Well-being Trend
      ↓
Explainable AI Signal
      ↓
Authorised Human Review
      ↓
Intervention
      ↓
Follow-up
```

The interface should make this workflow understandable without requiring the user to understand machine-learning terminology.

# Visual Identity

Use:

**Primary:** `#702186`

**Supporting:** Dark Navy, White, Neutral Grey, Soft Purple

Use colour semantically rather than decoratively.

For example:

* Purple → SAHAYA-AI brand / primary actions
* Neutral → normal information
* Amber → attention / review
* Red → genuinely urgent system state
* Green → completed / improving / resolved

Do not use colour as the only method of communicating status.

---

# The Core Design Philosophy

SAHAYA-AI should **not look like an AI demo**.

It should look like a **real victim-support system that happens to use AI behind the scenes**.

The user should immediately understand:

> **What is happening?**
> **Why does it matter?**
> **What can I do next?**
> **Who can see this information?**

Every visual element, animation, chart, card, button, and piece of copy must serve one of those purposes.

**Do not add a design element simply because it is currently popular in AI-generated websites.**

Every element must have a clear functional, informational, accessibility, trust, or safety purpose.
